# Changes

| 2026-10-15 | Second edition. Separated the reader's narrative (field, problem, ideas, construction, evidence) from the writer's analysis (claims, tests, ledgers), which change the work and never appear in the prose. Added a third failure pole, Defense, and named the design-led and deduction-driven variants of Catalog and Vapor. Replaced the stance "write against a belief" with "start from the field's problem; know what the work changes", and added the stance "defend in the work, not in the prose". Added stage 2, formulating the research problem from the field (problem vs means, domain vs object, constructive categories for prior work), renumbering the stages to nine. Rewrote the middle layer as observed difficulties rather than derived requirements, and the guidance on examples, scope, limitations, related work, naming, titles, re-conceiving rejected drafts, unfinished evaluations and briefs to other writers. Added `references/constructive-narrative.md` and a narrative-and-voice symptom group in `references/diagnostics.md` | An author's reviews of successive agent-written drafts of one systems paper, which rejected design-led, deduction-driven, defensive and conceptually packaged drafts and asked for problem-first narratives grounded in the field's literature; and an audit of the first edition against those reviews, which found that its checks, read literally, would not prevent those failures and in places invite them (see [references/origins.md](references/origins.md)) | The opponent-first framing; the middle layer as "what any solution must satisfy"; scoping out alternatives and inline-and-aggregated caveats as defaults; the running example chosen to exercise every mechanism; the two-pole picture of failure |
